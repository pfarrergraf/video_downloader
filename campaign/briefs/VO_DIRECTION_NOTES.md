# Voice direction notes — concepts 3 & 5

Generated via the `voiceover-direction` skill. Covers the two closing VO
lines that pair with the finished rough-cuts in `campaign/selected/`.

## Concept 5 — "Three free downloads a day. No account required. Mission complete."

**Intention to give the talent (not a line reading):** You just calmly
defused a bomb that was, in reality, a "create an account" popup. You're not
in on the joke with the audience — you play this completely straight, like an
actual mission debrief. The comedy comes from the mismatch between your
seriousness and how small the stakes actually are. If you play it knowing
it's funny, it dies.

**Tone:** Deadpan mission-debrief. Same register as a movie's tactical radio
voice — flat, controlled, zero irony.

**Pacing:** Measured, three distinct beats, not one rushed sentence:
- "Three free downloads a day." (state it as a known fact, not a pitch)
- [tiny beat]
- "No account required." (land on "required" — that's the payoff word)
- [slightly longer beat]
- "Mission complete." (flat, quiet, almost bored — the anticlimax IS the joke)

**Emphasis:** "No" and "required" carry the line — not "account." Emphasizing
"account" makes it sound like a sales pitch; emphasizing "required" makes it
sound like a debrief confirming a condition was met.

**Breath placement:** One breath before "Three free downloads," full stop
breath before "Mission complete" — that pause is doing the comedic work, give
it room.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't smile through it or let any warmth/friendliness creep in — this is
  not a friendly customer-service VO.
- Don't add a knowing chuckle or wink-to-camera inflection anywhere.
- Don't rush "mission complete" to get to the end — that's the punchline,
  it needs the same weight as the rest.

**Casting:** Male or female, vocal age 25-40, low-to-mid register, the kind
of voice you'd cast for a movie-trailer tactical-ops radio call. Energy 3/10
— controlled, not sleepy. Avoid anyone whose natural read defaults to
"upbeat commercial" — that instinct will fight this line the whole session.

## Concept 3 — "Yeah. That's it."

**Intention to give the talent:** You've just watched someone (yourself, in
the bit) go through an absurd amount of trouble to lose the same video three
times, and the fourth time it just... worked. No fanfare. You are almost
disappointed there's nothing more to say. This is the "well, that was
easy" feeling stripped of all enthusiasm.

**Tone:** Bored, matter-of-fact, faintly amused at how undramatic it is.
Think of someone confirming something obvious to a friend who's still waiting
for a twist that isn't coming.

**Pacing:** Slow and loose, with real dead air around it — this line should
feel underproduced, almost like a throwaway aside, not a scripted VO tag.
- "Yeah." (trail off slightly, don't commit to it like an answer)
- [longer-than-comfortable pause]
- "That's it." (flat, slight downward inflection, no button/sting energy)

**Emphasis:** Almost none — that's the point. If anything, the softest
emphasis lands on "it," but underplayed, not punched.

**Breath placement:** Let the pause between "Yeah." and "That's it." run
longer than feels natural in a normal read — the discomfort of the silence is
what sells the anticlimax.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't play it as a punchline or add a laugh/smirk in the voice — the moment
  it sounds like a joke being told, it stops being funny.
- Don't oversell the relief — no relieved exhale, no warmth, no "finally!"
  energy.
- Don't speed up to fill the silence — the silence is the joke, not dead air
  to edit around.

**Casting:** Vocal age 20-35, casual/conversational register (not
broadcast-polished), the kind of voice that sounds like it just rolled out of
bed. Energy 2/10. This is the opposite casting instinct from concept 5 —
don't use the same talent for both without checking they can actually drop
the "mission debrief" control and go fully loose/bored for this one; those
are different muscles.

## Cross-concept note
These two lines should NOT be read by directing the same energy twice. #5 is
controlled deadpan (withholding emotion on purpose); #3 is loose deadpan
(genuinely can't be bothered). If one talent is doing both, the session
should explicitly reset between them — treat them as two different
characters, not two reads of "the DownloadThat! voice."
